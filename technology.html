Architecture

Technology

A few parts work together. The architecture is still being finalised.

Status: first-revision prototype, not yet tested in a building.

Cutaway

Look inside the building

Part

Load-management unit

In the proposed design, each of our chargers combines a Raspberry Pi 5 computer, which runs the supervisory software, with our own control board and wireless-mesh radio in one enclosure. A building-level allocator shares capacity. Where it runs is not yet decided.

In short: It decides how much current each car gets.

Rationale: A widely available computer keeps decisions local, so they should work without the internet.

Part

Charger control electronics

The control board in our charger talks to the vehicle through the control pilot signal, measures power and switches the charging relay. It is designed to follow the SAE J1772 and IEC 61851 control-pilot standards for AC charging.

In short: Talks to the car, measures power, switches charging.

Rationale: One built-in board carries the control signal and the meter.

Part

Metering

Each charger measures its own energy, voltage, current and power. The building's total is measured too and passed to the unit, which shares capacity under the building limit. Values are reported to the back office over OCPP 1.6.

Required accuracy and reporting rate are not yet decided.

In short: The system knows what each charger and the building use.

Rationale: The unit can only hold the building under its limit if it sees what is drawn.

Part

Wireless mesh

Our chargers talk over a wireless mesh, with the radio built in. It needs no mobile plan and no building Wi-Fi. Its longer-range link should reach between floors, but range is not yet proven in a building.

In short: Chargers pass messages along for their neighbours.

Rationale: A mesh is intended to reach across floors without new data cables and route around a failed node. Coverage in real garages is still to be tested.

Part

OCPP backend link

The system reports sessions and meter values to a charging network operator over OCPP 1.6, and supports remote start and stop. Local control is designed not to depend on this link.

In short: How chargers report to an operator.

Rationale: OCPP 1.6 is open, so the system is not tied to one operator.

Part

Safety

Software test results alone never clear a real charging session. Simulated, bench and system-level validation are kept separate.

In short: Passing tests never permits charging a real car.

Rationale: Separate levels tie each claim to its evidence until proven on a real installation.
