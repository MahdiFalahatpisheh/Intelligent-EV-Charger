Operation

How it works

The system repeats three steps in a continuous loop. Each pass re-reads the building, so the plan keeps up as cars arrive, finish and leave.

Resilience

If the internet fails

Illustration of the design: charging continues, and reports are sent when the connection returns.

Sense

Each charger measures how much power its car draws. The load-management unit also accounts for the building's other electrical load, such as lighting, lifts and ventilation. This shows how much room is left for charging. The unit also tracks which vehicles are plugged in and which have finished.

Share capacity

The load-management unit divides the capacity left after the building's other loads among plugged-in vehicles. It adjusts each charger's current limit through the standard charging control signal, which compatible vehicles already understand. When there is not enough for everyone, charging can be paused or rotated. The sharing policy is still to be defined.

See why the building limit matters

Charge

Each charger delivers the current it is allotted, and the vehicle follows that limit. The load-management unit keeps measuring and adjusting, aiming to hold total demand within the building's permitted limit as conditions change. A charger can pause a session when no capacity is available, then resume when it returns.

Works without the cloud

Allocation runs locally. The goal is that charging continues safely if the internet or cloud connection is lost, with data buffered until the connection returns. A fallback for a charger that loses contact with the load-management unit is planned. Its behaviour is still being defined.

Chargers and the load-management unit are linked by a local wireless mesh inside the building, so sharing does not depend on the internet. The mesh arrangement is not yet decided.

Outage

When the internet drops

Picture an evening when many residents have just arrived home. The building's internet connection then fails.

By design, the chargers keep working. Capacity sharing runs on the load-management unit inside the building, not in the cloud. It does not need the internet to decide who gets how much current.

Meter readings and session records are stored locally and sent to the charging network operator once the connection returns. Remote start and stop from the operator wait until then.

If one charger loses contact with the load-management unit, it falls back to a safe current until the link is restored. The value and the time allowed are still to be decided. The aim is that the building limit is still respected.

Status: prototype in development. This describes the design and has not yet been validated on a real installation.
