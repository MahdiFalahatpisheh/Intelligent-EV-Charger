Operation

How it works

Three steps repeat in a loop, so charging keeps up as cars arrive, finish and leave.

Resilience

If the internet fails

Illustration of the design: charging continues, and reports are sent when the connection returns.

Step

Sense

Each charger is designed to measure the power its car draws. The load-management unit is designed to add the building's other load, such as lighting and lifts, to see how much room is left. It is also designed to track which vehicles are plugged in or finished.

Read about metering

Step

Share capacity

The unit is designed to divide spare capacity among plugged-in vehicles, setting each charger's limit through the standard charging control signal. When there is not enough, charging can be paused or rotated. The sharing policy is still to be defined.

Every building has a power limit. If many residents plug in at once, uncontrolled charging can pass it.

See why the building limit matters

Step

Charge

Each charger is designed to deliver its allotted current. The unit is designed to keep measuring, aiming to hold demand within the building's limit. A charger can pause a session when no capacity is available, then resume.

Resilience

Works without the cloud

Allocation is designed to run locally. The goal is that charging continues safely without the internet, with data buffered until it returns. A fallback for a charger that loses the unit is planned; its behaviour is still being defined.

Show the mesh and install detail

Our chargers and the unit are designed to be linked by a local wireless mesh, so sharing is designed to work without the internet. The arrangement is not yet decided.

Wired setups can mean new cable to every charger, and a break means repairing the route. Our charger is designed to be fixed to the wall and paired to the mesh, so adding or moving one needs no new data cable. Quicker install and easier upkeep are aims, not yet measured.

Outage

When the internet drops

Picture an evening when many residents arrive home and the internet fails.

By design, the chargers keep working. Sharing is designed to run on the unit inside the building, not in the cloud.

Meter readings and session records are designed to be stored locally and sent when the connection returns. Remote start and stop wait until then.

A charger that loses contact with the unit is designed to fall back to a safe current until the link returns. The value and time allowed are still to be decided. The aim is to respect the building limit.

Status: prototype in development. This is the design, not yet validated on a real installation.
