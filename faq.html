Frequently asked questions

What problem does this solve?

Apartment buildings often lack the capacity for every resident to charge at full power at once. The system shares the building's existing capacity instead of needing an upgrade.

Full speed for every car?

No. When capacity is short, some vehicles may be slowed, paused or queued. The policy is still to be defined. The system aims to keep total demand within the building's limit.

What does the system measure?

Each charger measures its own energy, voltage, current and power. The building's base load is measured too, so the system knows what is left. Still to be decided: the building meter, required accuracy, reporting rate, and whether readings suit billing. See the metering section.

If the internet goes down?

Allocation runs on the local load-management unit, designed to continue without the internet. Data is stored locally and sent when the connection returns. A charger that loses contact with the unit falls back to a safe current; the value and timeout are not yet decided.

Do chargers need data cables?

The proposed design links chargers and the load-management unit over a wireless mesh, which is intended to avoid new data cables.

How do chargers talk?

Our chargers and the load-management unit form their own wireless network. Chargers pass messages along for their neighbours, so a distant one can still be reached. The unit sends each its share, and chargers send readings back. The arrangement is not yet decided. See how it works.

Which standards does it follow?

Our charger is designed to follow the SAE J1772 and IEC 61851 control-pilot standards for AC charging, and uses OCPP 1.6 for the backend link. The full list of standards and certifications is still being confirmed.

Is it ready to install?

Not yet. It is a prototype in development with working-draft requirements, and has not been validated on a real installation.

Does it need a cloud service?

No. Local control is designed not to depend on the cloud. The OCPP link to a charging network operator is optional for local operation, used for reporting and remote start and stop.

Solar, batteries, vehicle-to-grid?

These are possible later work. They are not in the first version.
