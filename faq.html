Frequently asked questions

What problem does this solve?

Apartment buildings often lack the electrical capacity for every resident to charge at full power at once. The system shares the capacity the building already has, as an alternative to an upgrade.

Will every car always charge at full speed?

No. When capacity is short, some vehicles may be slowed, paused or queued. The policy is still to be defined. The system aims to keep total demand within the building's permitted limit.

What does the system measure?

Each charger measures the energy, voltage, current and power it delivers. The building's own base load is measured too, so the system knows how much capacity is left for charging. Still to be decided: which meter supplies the building figure, how accurate readings must be, how often they are reported, and whether they will be good enough for billing. See the metering section.

What happens if the internet goes down?

Allocation runs on the local load-management unit, and local control is designed to continue without an internet connection. Data is stored locally and sent when the connection returns. A charger that loses contact with the load-management unit falls back to a safe current. The value and the timeout are not yet decided.

Do the chargers need new data cables?

The proposed design links chargers and the load-management unit over a wireless mesh, which is intended to avoid new data cables.

How do the chargers talk to each other?

The chargers and the load-management unit form their own wireless network. Each charger passes messages along for its neighbours, so a charger far from the unit can still be reached. The unit sends each charger its share of the capacity over this network, and the chargers send their readings back. How they are arranged is not yet decided. See how it works.

Which standards does it follow?

The charger board is designed to follow the SAE J1772 and IEC 61851 control-pilot standards for AC charging, and the backend link uses OCPP 1.6. The full list of standards and certifications is still being confirmed.

Is it ready to install?

Not yet. It is a prototype in development, with a first board revision and working-draft requirements. It has not been validated on a real installation.

Does it need a cloud service?

No. Local control is designed not to depend on the cloud. The OCPP link to a charging network operator is optional for local operation. It is used for reporting and remote start and stop.

What about solar, batteries or vehicle-to-grid?

These are possible later work. They are not in the first version.
