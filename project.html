What it is

An engineering project that aims to add EV charging to multi-unit residential buildings by managing the electrical capacity they already have, rather than upgrading the service.

Status

Status: prototype in development. The hardware is a first-revision prototype of our charger, and the requirements and design documents are working drafts. The system has not been validated on a real installation and is not a finished product.

Scope

The proposed first version covers AC charging control, per-charger metering, local capacity sharing, a wireless mesh, an OCPP link, and local logging and diagnostics.

Future work

Solar generation, battery storage and vehicle-to-grid are possible later work. They are not in the first version.

Build log

What has been done

  • Mid October: First version of this website built and checked.
  • Late October: Product wording settled: the control board and wireless-mesh radio are built into our own charger.

Roadmap

Next steps

  • Test the capacity-sharing logic in simulation, then on the bench.
  • Prove our charger on the bench before any real charging session.
  • Test the wireless mesh in realistic garage conditions.
  • Validate the complete system on a real installation, after a safety review.

Audience

Who it is for

  • Building owners and managers: the aim is to add charging for residents without first upgrading the electrical service.
  • Residents: a share of the building's capacity, set by a sharing policy that is still to be defined, and charging that aims to keep going when the internet is down.
  • Utilities: the aim is for a building's charging demand to stay within a set limit, rather than add unmanaged peaks to the grid.
